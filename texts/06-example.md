## A short example

The people here are invented. This shows the shape of a session, not words to copy; do not use these names, roles or answers for a real session.

**Event:** Teams piloting AI assistants at work. Purpose: people running a pilot this quarter compare what they measure and what went wrong.

**The person's answers**

- One-line introduction: Marketing lead running a two-week-old pilot of an AI assistant that drafts product briefs.
- What can you offer this event? An anonymised copy of the scoring sheet we used in the first two weeks, and what we changed after it.
- What would make one conversation worthwhile? Someone who has rolled out a similar assistant to at least two teams. I want to ask how they catch wrong answers before users do.

**One introduction from the list**

> **You should meet Selin.** · Strong
> Support operations lead at a mid-sized software company; looks after the support assistant.
>
> **Why you two**
> What they bring you: Selin reviews assistant answers across two teams with a checklist, which is the step your pilot has not built yet.
> What you bring them: she suspects her team's mistakes are not special; your scoring sheet from a different department is the comparison she is missing.
>
> **Open with:** "Which kind of wrong answer does your checklist catch that people reading the answer usually miss?"

**Two turns, busy mood**

> Person: Hi Selin, thanks for making time. I am doing lots of things with AI and wanted to hear your thoughts.
>
> Selin: Hi. I have a few minutes. Which part?

**From the debrief**

- Two things to change: you opened with "lots of things with AI" although the introduction had given you the reason. You never mentioned the scoring sheet, which is what she wanted.
- Say it this way: "Hi Selin. We were introduced because you review assistant answers across two teams and I am two weeks into a pilot. I can show you our scoring sheet; which wrong answers does your checklist catch first?"
- Result: 2 of 5: question, listening.
